The National Curriculum publishes KS4 programmes of study for **English, mathematics and science** (and for citizenship, computing and PE) — Department for Education (2014), *National curriculum in England: key stages 3 and 4 framework document*. For those subjects the canonical KS4 nodes anchor on the real programme of study, exactly as the KS3 nodes do, and carry `nc_ref` anchors where the Oak ontology provides them.

For every other subject (humanities, creative arts, digital literacy) there is no KS4 programme of study: what counts as the curriculum is the awarding-body specifications in aggregate. There the vault treats canonical KS4 nodes as **frames defined by the union of overlays that point at them**. In both cases the overlay layer does the same work — it shows which qualification tests what.

A canonical KS4 English Reading node, for example, names the broad area of learning in plain English ("comprehension of unseen non-fiction texts, including 19th–21st century literary prose") and surfaces the overlay outcomes that test it — Edexcel IGCSE 4EA1 AO1, AQA GCSE English Language AO1, Pearson Edexcel FS English L2 R1 — with wikilink annotations showing which overlay tests what.

The result is navigable two ways: top-down (here's an area of KS4 English; here's how each overlay approaches it) and bottom-up (here's an Edexcel spec point; here's the canonical area; here are the AQA and FS equivalents). What learning *is* at KS4 becomes the consensus across overlays, made visible.
